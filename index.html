// Structural Panel Design ＋

Week-long Boeing Structures training project: designed a vent-duct penetration through a structural panel, with its bracket, doubler, and fasteners, then built and installed it on a mock structure.

  1. RequirementsLoads, geometry, and design criteria from the coordination sheet
  2. Solution & AnalysisBracket bending, doubler sizing, and Hi-Lok fastener layout
  3. Testing & Installation3D-printed bracket installed on a mock aircraft structure
Full methodology ＋

01 Requirements

  • Pulled the penetration, bracket, applied loads, and surrounding geometry from the coordination sheet.
  • Identified missing inputs: web and bracket thickness, material properties, and allowable stresses.
  • Applied design criteria for fastener pitch, edge margin, clearance, and reinforcement, and separated fixed geometry from what could move.

02 Solution & Analysis

  • Bracket: traced the vent load path, calculated bending moment and section properties, and checked bending stress against the allowable.
  • Doubler: sized a local doubler from the running load interrupted by the penetration to restore the web’s load path.
  • Fasteners: laid out Hi-Lok fasteners for pitch, edge margin, and clearance, reusing existing holes where possible.
  • Iteration: adjusted penetration location, bracket, doubler, and fasteners together until every check passed.

03 Testing & Installation

  • 3D-printed the bracket and installed the bracket, doubler, fasteners, and penetration on a mock aircraft structure.
  • Learned how manufacturability, tolerances, access, and assembly sequence shape a design.

Scope: a one-week training exercise with simplified inputs. A production modification would add fatigue and damage-tolerance analysis, more failure modes, corrosion protection, inspection planning, and formal cross-functional review before certification.
