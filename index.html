Structural Panel Design Exercise — Fastener Selection & Load Path ＋

A week-long design exercise: a coordination request asks for a duct penetration and support bracket to be added to an existing riveted structural panel, which then has to be re-analyzed for the added hole and the bracket's attachment.

Re-drawn from memory as a generic sketch — dimensions and the original coordination sheet are not reproduced.

Approach

  1. Loads in. Take the applied bracket load and resolve it into the running shear flow the fasteners around the cutout have to carry.
  2. Fastener type. Compare a Hi-Lok against a rivet at the joint — Hi-Loks carry more load per fastener and don't loosen under vibration, but cost more and take longer to install; rivets are cheaper and faster for lightly loaded skin joints. Diameter is sized from the running load per fastener against the manufacturer's shear allowable for a given fastener material and diameter, with margin.
  3. Fastener spacing. Space fasteners along the hole's tangent line using a pitch derived from the running load, with a knockdown factor (roughly 1.5×) applied to keep bearing and net-section stresses within standard aerospace joint-efficiency limits — this sets how many fasteners fit between the tangent lines.
  4. Geometry checks. Verify edge margin, end margin, fastener pitch, and fastener-to-part-edge distance all fall within the standard aerospace ranges for the sheet thickness and fastener diameter used (commonly expressed as ratios of hole diameter, e.g. e/D, W/D).
  5. Bending check on the bracket. Treat the bracket leg as a simple cantilever/beam under the applied load and check bending stress with σ = Mc / I against the material allowable, alongside the fastener bearing and shear-out checks at the bracket attach holes.

This mirrors the general method taught across aerospace structures references (e.g. Bruhn, Niu) — specific Boeing-manual criteria, allowables, and figures are intentionally left out.

ME 2110 — Creative Decisions & Design: "Train Your Dragon" Competition Robot ＋

Semester-long capstone-style team project (with Franchesca Clegg, Catherine Thurston, and Christopher Lucas) to design, build, and iterate a competition robot completing five tasks: launch and deploy, extinguish a fire, refill feeding stations, return sheep to paddocks, and light a bonfire.

Subsystems

  • Drivetrain — dual-axle, belt-and-gear driven; iterated after wire drag and belt tension were costing wheel friction.
  • Sheep dispenser — went through three iterations (ramp turnstile → vertical dispenser → piston-actuated vertical dispenser) chasing a 95% reliable single-sheep release, root-causing each jam before redesigning.
  • Sweeping "wing" mechanism — hinged, spring-loaded arms released by a locking rod to sweep torches onto the target.
  • Three-stage telescoping arm — concentric PVC pipes linked by nylon thread and a motor-driven spool, to extend and flip a lever roughly 42" away.

Process

Used a function tree to translate competition tasks into engineering requirements (e.g. "Extinguish Fire" became "Activate Lever"), then a morph chart to compare candidate mechanisms before committing to CAD and fabrication — for example, weighing a simple mechanical turnstile against a pressurized piston system for the sheep task before building and testing both.

Results

Scored above the field median in each sprint (own score ~50–70 vs. a median of ~40 in Sprint 2; ~70 vs ~70 in Sprint 3) and reached 95%+ task completion by final competition week, ultimately eliminated in the Sweet 16.
